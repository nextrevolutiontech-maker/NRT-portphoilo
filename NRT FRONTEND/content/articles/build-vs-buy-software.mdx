# Build vs Buy Software

Every growing enterprise eventually faces the ultimate IT dilemma: Do we purchase an existing SaaS subscription (Buy), or do we hire developers to build a proprietary system from scratch (Build)? The wrong choice results in either massive technical debt or bloated operational expenses.

## When to Buy (Off-The-Shelf)

You should buy software when the business process is a **commodity**.
There is zero competitive advantage in building your own email server or standard payroll calculator. Tools like Google Workspace, Slack, or QuickBooks exist because those processes are identical across 99% of businesses. Buying is faster, cheaper, and comes with immediate support.

## When to Build (Custom Software)

You should build software when the process is your **competitive advantage** or when you operate in a highly specialized niche.
For example, if you run a logistics company with a proprietary method for packing and routing trucks that saves you 20% on fuel compared to competitors, no off-the-shelf software will support that exact workflow. If you force your team to use generic software, you lose your competitive edge.

## The Licensing Cost Trap

Many CEOs choose SaaS because the upfront cost is low ($50/user/month). However, they fail to calculate the Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) over 5 years as their team scales. 
If you have 100 employees, that SaaS tool costs $60,000 annually. Over 5 years, you've paid $300,000 and you still own nothing. A custom build might cost $80,000 upfront, but it requires zero per-user licenses, making it significantly cheaper at scale while acting as an IP asset for your company's valuation.
