# What is a FinTech Platform?

Financial Technology (FinTech) is no longer a buzzword; it is the foundational infrastructure of modern commerce. From the app you use to split a dinner bill to the automated systems processing enterprise payroll, FinTech platforms are dismantling traditional banking monopolies and replacing them with agile, API-driven software.

## The Core Components of FinTech

While "FinTech" covers a massive industry, most enterprise platforms share a common architectural DNA:

1.  **The Core Ledger:** The immutable database where all financial truths are stored. Unlike standard databases, a financial ledger is append-only, ensuring absolute auditability of every cent moved.
2.  **Payment Gateways:** The APIs that securely transmit credit card or bank account data between the user, the merchant, and the acquiring bank (e.g., Stripe, PayPal).
3.  **Digital Identity & KYC:** Automated systems that verify a user's identity using government databases and facial recognition before allowing them to transact, ensuring compliance with Anti-Money Laundering (AML) laws.

## Why Legacy Banks are Partnering with FinTechs

Traditional banks run on mainframe computer systems built in the 1980s. They are stable but incredibly difficult to update. FinTech startups, on the other hand, are built on cloud-native microservices. Today, instead of competing, banks provide the regulatory license and capital, while FinTechs provide the modern mobile app and user experience—a concept known as Banking-as-a-Service (BaaS).

## Security First

Building a FinTech platform requires a fundamentally different engineering mindset. A bug in a social media app is an inconvenience; a bug in a FinTech app is a financial catastrophe.
